# High-Speed Impact Study

## Overview

An event lasting milliseconds expands into readable phases of deformation, fracture, splash, or recoil.

## Metadata

- Collection: Experimental Camera and Image Processes
- Medium: HTML, SVG, Canvas, WebGL, or composited media
- Best for: physics, products, sports, and decisive moments
- Complexity: Moderate to advanced

## Signature Features

- Use a plain calibrated background, one impact subject, crisp short exposure, deformation detail, and optional timing marks.
- Expand milliseconds into ordered phases—approach, contact, compression, fracture or splash, recoil—then compare the intact and final states.
- For **High-Speed Impact Study**, use a palette, contrast curve, grain structure, and edge behavior native to the named capture or darkroom process.

## Avoid

- Avoid slow-motion blur, repeated impacts from different setups, exaggerated explosions, handheld camera, or impossible material response.
- Do not imitate a photographic, optical, or compression process with unrelated glitch decoration or a generic color filter.
- Keep essential text readable and provide a stable reduced-motion state that preserves the final composition.
