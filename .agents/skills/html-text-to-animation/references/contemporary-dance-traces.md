# Contemporary Dance Traces

## Overview

A dancer’s weight, reach, contact, and pathways leave temporary spatial marks without replacing the body.

## Metadata

- Collection: Performance, Ritual and Living Culture
- Medium: HTML, SVG, Canvas, WebGL, or composited media
- Best for: emotion, choreography, relationships, and embodied movement

## Signature Features

- Keep the dancer fully visible in a simple space; let traces encode weight transfer, reach, floor path, contact, or duration.
- Draw marks after motion establishes them, allow traces to decay by age, and use a stable wide camera with rare motivated cuts.
- For **Contemporary Dance Traces**, treat stage, material, performer, audience viewpoint, and timing as one coherent form; use culturally specific features only when they are verified and relevant.

## Style Prompt

Create a polished HTML animation in the **Contemporary Dance Traces** style. Draw marks after motion establishes them, allow traces to decay by age, and use a stable wide camera with rare motivated cuts. Treat stage, material, performer, audience viewpoint, and timing as one coherent form; verify culturally specific features before using them. Keep typography in respectful editorial framing or authentic program-like contexts, never over faces, gestures, sacred objects, or primary craft detail. Let the performance's entrance, gesture, rhythm, object handling, and audience relationship determine transitions instead of using generic spectacle.

## Avoid

- Avoid neon ribbons replacing the body, random motion capture skeletons, constant slow motion, close crops, or traces unrelated to force.
- Do not imitate cultural identity through generic ornament, invented symbols, unrelated regional motifs, or mixed traditions.
