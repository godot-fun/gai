# Northern Renaissance Material Portrait

## Overview

A formal half-length portrait where layered oil glazes make velvet, fur, metal, leather, and flesh materially distinct.

## Style Prompt

Present any subject as a formal half-length sitter wearing clothing appropriate to its own world. Build the image in traditional layers: colored ground, directional opaque modeling, transparent glazes, then restrained fine detail. Give every material its own grammar—individual tapered hairs in fur, sharp light ridges along velvet folds, two compact specular points on each metal object, and diffuse nap on wool or leather. Use deep green drapery, warm near-black, wine velvet, aged gold, and fur brown around fully modeled warm skin. Light softly from upper left so the face retains volume and the background stays deep but legible. Explain the wider setting through a small window or one object held in the sitter's hands rather than a narrative collage. Finish with warm varnish, fine craquelure, and only faint visible brush in cloth and backdrop. Begin on the half portrait, cut to a meaningful material detail, then pull slowly into the surrounding dark room and its small pool of warm light.

## Customizable Elements

Adapt the subject, setting, wording, duration, aspect ratio, and narrative emphasis. Adjust individual hues only within the palette roles defined above, and preserve the medium, composition, material behavior, and motion grammar that make the style recognizable.

## Avoid

flat fills, hard digital edges, uniform gloss, plastic skin, empty costume symbolism, and faces without dimensional structure.
