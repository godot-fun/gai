# Charcoal Fog Memory

## Overview

A tactile charcoal world where darkness is built into the paper and fog is erased back out of it.

## Metadata

- Collection: Academy Award Film Styles
- Medium: Cinematic HTML animation
- Source type: visual style reference

## Signature Features

- Toothed paper with pressure-sensitive charcoal grain
- Velvet blacks, broad side-stick sweeps, smudging, and clean eraser lifts
- Layered fog bands moving at different speeds
- One dense pastel focal subject
- Slow subjective travel through parting foregrounds

## Style Prompt

Tone most of the sheet before drawing so the image grows out of darkness rather than floating on white. Use vine charcoal for broken contours, the side of a charcoal stick for broad form-following masses, sparse hatch for structure, finger-smudged transitions, and kneaded-eraser lifts for rims, mist, and reflected light. Let paper tooth show only in middle values; press deeply enough for dark passages to become velvety and leave highlights as clean paper. Keep roughly three fifths of the frame between dark grey and black, with the deepest masses nearest the viewer. Build fog by horizontally erasing two or three bands from the laid tone and move them at unequal speeds. Give only the central subject a compact, fairly opaque pastel color that still catches the tooth; outline it again in charcoal. Fire and lamps remain black-and-paper abstractions. Move forward slowly from a subjective viewpoint while foreground branches, curtains, or architecture separate to either side.

## Customizable Elements

Adapt the subject, setting, wording, duration, aspect ratio, and narrative emphasis. Adjust individual hues only within the palette roles defined above, and preserve the medium, composition, material behavior, and motion grammar that make the style recognizable.

## Avoid

digital noise, airbrushed glow, weak grey blacks, and large unused bright skies.
