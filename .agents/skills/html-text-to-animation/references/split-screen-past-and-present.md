# Split-Screen Past and Present

## Overview

A moving divider holds two synchronized worlds in tension: then and now, parent and child, cold and warm.

## Metadata

- Collection: Code Motion Studies
- Medium: HTML, Canvas, SVG, or WebGL
- Best for: before-and-after stories, generational contrast, and opposing interpretations
- Complexity: Moderate

## Signature Features

- Both sides share comparable scale and hierarchy.
- A clear divider acts as both boundary and transition.
- Visual differences are revealed in synchronized beats.

## Style Prompt

Create a polished HTML animation in the **Split-Screen Past and Present** style. Compose two matched scenes with contrasting but related palettes. Let the divider shift subtly as emphasis changes, synchronize corresponding events, and use mirrored text placement so comparison remains immediate.

Adapt the subject, wording, duration, aspect ratio, and rendering technique to the user's material. Preserve the defining behavior above and keep all supplied text unchanged unless rewriting is requested.

## Customizable Elements

- Subject matter, on-screen wording, duration, aspect ratio, typeface, and accent color.
- Rendering may use HTML, CSS, SVG, Canvas, or WebGL according to the needs of the effect.

## Avoid

- Unmatched framing, unequal reading time, unrelated compositions, decorative split screens, or a divider that obscures content.
- Do not reproduce historical sample copy or hard-coded layouts from the source material.
- Do not sacrifice readability for motion density.
