# Pushed 16mm Pursuit

## Overview

A detailed 3D world degraded into urgent early-1970s street reportage through low lenses, clustered grain, and obstructed motion.

## Metadata

- Collection: Academy Award Film Styles
- Medium: Cinematic HTML animation
- Source type: visual style reference

## Signature Features

- Low saturation and hard contrast without an all-blue cast
- Near-white overcast sky and warm brick, timber, and vehicle browns
- Cyan-leaning shadows, gate weave, flicker, dust, and highlight ghosts
- Dense location-specific clutter
- Low wide-angle handheld tracking through repeated foreground obstacles

## Style Prompt

Construct the location with enough three-dimensional specificity to withstand documentary scrutiny: signage, pipes, rails, parked vehicles, grime, cables, bins, fixtures, and objects that belong to the place. Grade it like pushed 16mm: low saturation, strong contrast, neutral cool greys, cyan-leaning darks, and persistent warm brown in brick, wood, skin, and vehicles across at least a quarter of the frame. Keep an overcast sky close to white at any hour. Where an elevated track, bridge, grating, or pier exists, use it to cast hard repeating stripes across the action. Add clustered grain large enough to feel photochemical but not obscure edges, plus gate weave, exposure flutter, dust, and occasional pale highlight ghosting. Keep the focal subject more saturated than its surroundings. Chase the action from a low eighteen-to-twenty-five-millimeter viewpoint, allowing poles, columns, sleepers, or fence bars to strobe past; counter with a backward-facing track and one wide corner view.

## Customizable Elements

Adapt the subject, setting, wording, duration, aspect ratio, and narrative emphasis. Adjust individual hues only within the palette roles defined above, and preserve the medium, composition, material behavior, and motion grammar that make the style recognizable.

## Avoid

polished CG reflections, empty blockout streets, blanket teal grading, smeared noise, and stabilized commercial camera movement.
