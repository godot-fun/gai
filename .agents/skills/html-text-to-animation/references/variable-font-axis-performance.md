# Variable-Font Axis Performance

## Overview

Weight, width, slant, optical size, and custom font axes animate as semantic dimensions rather than simple scaling.

## Metadata

- Collection: Typography and Language Systems
- Medium: HTML, SVG, Canvas, WebGL, or composited media
- Best for: identity, emphasis, responsive typography, and vocal nuance
- Complexity: Moderate to advanced

## Signature Features

- Choose a genuine variable family and map weight, width, slant, optical size, or a custom axis to distinct semantic or acoustic changes.
- Interpolate axes continuously within valid ranges, hold readable extrema, and keep line breaks stable unless width change is the subject.
- For **Variable-Font Axis Performance**, build hierarchy from the writing system itself: baseline, spacing, glyph anatomy, reading direction, and linguistic timing take precedence over illustration.

## Style Prompt

Create a polished HTML animation in the **Variable-Font Axis Performance** style. Interpolate axes continuously within valid ranges, hold readable extrema, and keep line breaks stable unless width change is the subject. Build hierarchy from glyph anatomy, spacing, reading direction, linguistic timing, and the specific encoding or word structure being demonstrated. Limit color to paper or screen neutrals plus one semantic accent; keep every required character readable at the intended viewing distance. Let reading order, linguistic structure, or encoding rules determine transitions instead of applying generic kinetic-type presets.

Adapt duration, aspect ratio, hierarchy, and annotation without changing supplied wording. Verify every character, pronunciation, encoding, and reading convention used by the effect.

## Customizable Elements

- Adjust type family, scale, spacing, line length, pacing, annotation depth, and the balance between primary reading and structural explanation.
- Change typographic scale, neutral surface, accent color, and motion amplitude while preserving valid glyphs, reading order, timing, and decodability.

## Avoid

- Avoid scaling glyphs instead of changing axes, arbitrary wobble, mixing unrelated fonts, illegible extremes, or every axis moving at once.
- Do not invent characters, pronunciations, encodings, reading conventions, or linguistic relationships to complete the composition.
- Keep essential text readable and provide a stable reduced-motion state that preserves the final composition.
