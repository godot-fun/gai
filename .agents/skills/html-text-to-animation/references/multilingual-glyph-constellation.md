# Multilingual Glyph Constellation

## Overview

Related glyphs from multiple writing systems organize by sound, meaning, ancestry, or shape without treating them as decoration.

## Metadata

- Collection: Typography and Language Systems
- Medium: HTML, SVG, Canvas, WebGL, or composited media
- Best for: translation, language families, global stories, and comparative writing

## Signature Features

- Group verified glyphs by a declared relation—sound, meaning, ancestry, or form—while preserving each script's orientation and shaping rules.
- Introduce one writing system at a time, draw only defensible links, and let related clusters settle without force-layout jitter.
- For **Multilingual Glyph Constellation**, build hierarchy from the writing system itself: baseline, spacing, glyph anatomy, reading direction, and linguistic timing take precedence over illustration.

## Style Prompt

Create a polished HTML animation in the **Multilingual Glyph Constellation** style. Group verified glyphs by a declared relation—sound, meaning, ancestry, or form—while preserving each script's orientation and shaping rules. Introduce one writing system at a time, draw only defensible links, and let related clusters settle without force-layout jitter. Build hierarchy from glyph anatomy, spacing, reading direction, linguistic timing, and the specific encoding or word structure being demonstrated. Limit color to paper or screen neutrals plus one semantic accent; keep every required character readable at the intended viewing distance. Let reading order, linguistic structure, or encoding rules determine transitions instead of applying generic kinetic-type presets.

Adapt duration, aspect ratio, hierarchy, and annotation without changing supplied wording. Verify every character, pronunciation, encoding, and reading convention used by the effect.

## Avoid

- Avoid mixing scripts decoratively, invented etymologies, broken ligatures, romanization as a substitute, or visual similarity presented as shared origin.
- Do not invent characters, pronunciations, encodings, reading conventions, or linguistic relationships to complete the composition.
