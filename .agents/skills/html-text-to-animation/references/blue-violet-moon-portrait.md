# Blue-Violet Moon Portrait

## Overview

A calm night oil portrait modeled by blue moonlight, teal wet reflections, and a distant magenta rim while the sitter remains open-eyed and fully dimensional.

## Signature Features

- Smooth solid base volume repainted with two restrained brush passes
- Blue-violet environment occupying most of the frame
- Lavender moonlit planes, teal water light, and magenta distant rim
- Wet highlights on skin and visible eye reflections
- Two-thirds-height figure under a single full-moon key

## Style Prompt

Block the portrait as smooth, fully modeled volume, then repaint it with two passes of short form-following strokes. Use broader marks on sea, sky, land, and clothing and finer marks on face and hands, keeping stroke contrast subtle enough that anatomy remains calm. Assign middle blue-violet to sky, water, and distant land across most of the frame; lift moon-facing planes and sand toward pale lavender blue, place teal in water and secondary lit edges, and reserve muted magenta for a distant soft rim. Hold the deepest darks in blue-black rather than crushing them. Let any skin tone receive blue on upward planes, retain moist specular points, and keep open eyes alive with catchlights. Use the full moon as the only principal source, scattering broken reflections across water; minor practical lights remain tiny cyan-white dots. Frame the still figure at roughly two thirds of image height and push in slowly.

## Customizable Elements

Adapt the subject, setting, wording, duration, aspect ratio, and narrative emphasis. Adjust individual hues only within the palette roles defined above, and preserve the medium, composition, material behavior, and motion grammar that make the style recognizable.

## Avoid

chunky block strokes, black empty eyes, muddy near-black exposure, warm key light, many colored lamps, and agitation that contradicts the portrait's stillness.
