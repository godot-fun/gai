# Macro Fluid Photography

## Overview

Ink, oil, bubbles, and droplets collide at close range, revealing surface tension and microscopic turbulence.

## Metadata

- Collection: Experimental Camera and Image Processes
- Medium: HTML, SVG, Canvas, WebGL, or composited media
- Best for: abstract transitions, chemistry, beauty, and material interaction

## Signature Features

- Fill the frame with a small controlled fluid interaction—ink diffusion, oil-water cells, bubble films, or droplet collision—under directional macro light.
- Initiate one event, follow surface-tension boundaries at high temporal resolution, and avoid camera motion until scale is established.
- For **Macro Fluid Photography**, use a palette, contrast curve, grain structure, and edge behavior native to the named capture or darkroom process.

## Avoid

- Avoid multiple unrelated fluid effects, generic lava-lamp loops, turbulent speed, fake depth of field, or text submerged in busy flow.
- Do not imitate a photographic, optical, or compression process with unrelated glitch decoration or a generic color filter.
