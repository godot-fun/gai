# Single-Line Narrative

## Overview

A restrained one-line film in which one constant-width stroke draws, transforms, fills, and carries the viewer through a continuous chain of meanings. Choose this when one line connects multiple chronological scenes and carries a complete story arc.

## Metadata

- Collection: Motion Graphics Styles
- Medium: Cinematic HTML animation
- Source type: visual style reference

## Signature Features

- One or two colors and extensive negative space
- Constant-width round-capped line
- Visible draw-on synchronized to a traveling tip
- Previous image tail becomes next image beginning
- Brief line-to-filled-plane transformations at key moments

## Style Prompt

Design the entire sequence as one continuous path before animating it. Keep the stroke width constant, corners elegant, caps round, and the palette monochrome or duotone. Reveal the line from its moving tip at a speed matched to curvature, slowing slightly through intricate turns and accelerating across long straights. Make each completed object, figure, word, building, or diagram remain legible long enough before its final segment extends naturally into the next idea. Use generous blank space and let the virtual camera follow or anticipate the drawing tip without losing orientation. At one or two important moments, close a contour and let its fill expand from the anchor to become a solid plane, mask, or new scene, then return to line. Add only a tiny controlled hand tremor if appropriate.

## Customizable Elements

Adapt the subject, setting, wording, duration, aspect ratio, and narrative emphasis. Adjust individual hues only within the palette roles defined above, and preserve the medium, composition, material behavior, and motion grammar that make the style recognizable.

## Avoid

- Choose **Single-Line Landscape** for one contemplative environment, or **Continuous-Line Morph** for uninterrupted object metamorphosis.
disconnected paths disguised by cuts, variable line weights without purpose, crowded crossovers, decorative scribble, drawing faster than the viewer can parse, arbitrary zooms, and filled illustrations that abandon the one-line premise.
