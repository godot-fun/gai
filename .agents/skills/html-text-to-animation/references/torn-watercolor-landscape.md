# Torn Watercolor Landscape

## Overview

A calm scene assembled piece by piece from hand-torn watercolor paper, using strips for atmosphere, shards for detail, and exposed fibers as the main contour. Choose this when painted scenery is physically torn, layered, and transitioned through ragged paper edges.

## Signature Features

- Cold-press paper base
- Long torn strips for sky, sea, and terrain
- Small irregular shards for crowds, rocks, foliage, or buildings
- Visible white fibers, overlaps, and shallow shadows
- Measured placement and replacement rather than smooth morphing

## Style Prompt

Tint sheets of watercolor paper before tearing them, preserving granulation, blooms, and pigment variation within each scrap. Build broad sky, sea, and land from long horizontal strips; use smaller torn shards for rocks, crowds, trees, windows, or waves; cut no edge perfectly. Keep exposed white fibers visible and cast only shallow physical shadows where pieces overlap. Form a sun, moon, or focal symbol from a rough torn disc. Assemble the image gradually on a larger cold-press sheet, placing one meaningful layer at a time with calm timing. For changes, lift, slide, rotate, or replace scraps as paper; never liquefy them. Let a few loose fibers, paper edges, and pigment relationships carry motion while the composition remains warm and contemplative.
