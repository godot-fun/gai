# Circus Poster Spectacle

## Overview

Ornamental display type, painted acts, banners, stars, and sequential reveals turn promotion into theatrical motion.

## Metadata

- Collection: Performance, Ritual and Living Culture
- Medium: HTML, SVG, Canvas, WebGL, or composited media
- Best for: events, performers, exuberant titles, and nostalgic spectacle

## Signature Features

- Use a limited vintage ink palette, monumental display type, ornamental frames, painted act vignettes, and paper texture.
- Build the bill from headline to featured acts to date and venue, then animate poster elements with theatrical but print-aware reveals.
- For **Circus Poster Spectacle**, treat stage, material, performer, audience viewpoint, and timing as one coherent form; use culturally specific features only when they are verified and relevant.

## Style Prompt

Create a polished HTML animation in the **Circus Poster Spectacle** style. Use a limited vintage ink palette, monumental display type, ornamental frames, painted act vignettes, and paper texture. Build the bill from headline to featured acts to date and venue, then animate poster elements with theatrical but print-aware reveals. Treat stage, material, performer, audience viewpoint, and timing as one coherent form; verify culturally specific features before using them. Keep typography in respectful editorial framing or authentic program-like contexts, never over faces, gestures, sacred objects, or primary craft detail. Let the performance's entrance, gesture, rhythm, object handling, and audience relationship determine transitions instead of using generic spectacle.

## Avoid

- Avoid modern gradient branding, illegible ornamental overload, generic carnival clichés, copied historical performers, or every word moving independently.
- Do not imitate cultural identity through generic ornament, invented symbols, unrelated regional motifs, or mixed traditions.
