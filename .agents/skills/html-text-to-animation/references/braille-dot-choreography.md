# Braille Dot Choreography

## Overview

Six-dot cells rise and settle into valid Braille characters with tactile spacing and reading order.

## Metadata

- Collection: Typography and Language Systems
- Medium: HTML, SVG, Canvas, WebGL, or composited media
- Best for: accessibility, language systems, touch, and inclusive design
- Complexity: Moderate to advanced

## Signature Features

- Use valid six-dot or eight-dot cells, standard spacing, raised-dot lighting, and an adjacent transcription when needed.
- Raise dots cell by cell in reading order, trace touch direction subtly, and flatten between phrases only when the material permits.
- For **Braille Dot Choreography**, build hierarchy from the writing system itself: baseline, spacing, glyph anatomy, reading direction, and linguistic timing take precedence over illustration.

## Style Prompt

Create a polished HTML animation in the **Braille Dot Choreography** style. Use valid six-dot or eight-dot cells, standard spacing, raised-dot lighting, and an adjacent transcription when needed. Raise dots cell by cell in reading order, trace touch direction subtly, and flatten between phrases only when the material permits. Build hierarchy from glyph anatomy, spacing, reading direction, linguistic timing, and the specific encoding or word structure being demonstrated. Limit color to paper or screen neutrals plus one semantic accent; keep every required character readable at the intended viewing distance. Let reading order, linguistic structure, or encoding rules determine transitions instead of applying generic kinetic-type presets.

Adapt duration, aspect ratio, hierarchy, and annotation without changing supplied wording. Verify every character, pronunciation, encoding, and reading convention used by the effect.

## Customizable Elements

- Adjust type family, scale, spacing, line length, pacing, annotation depth, and the balance between primary reading and structural explanation.
- Change typographic scale, neutral surface, accent color, and motion amplitude while preserving valid glyphs, reading order, timing, and decodability.

## Avoid

- Avoid random dot matrices, decorative constellations, invalid contractions, inaccessible low contrast, or treating Braille as a cipher.
- Do not invent characters, pronunciations, encodings, reading conventions, or linguistic relationships to complete the composition.
- Keep essential text readable and provide a stable reduced-motion state that preserves the final composition.
