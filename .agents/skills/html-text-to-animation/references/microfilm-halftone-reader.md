# Microfilm Halftone Reader

## Overview

A low-contrast green newspaper photograph travels through a dusty microfilm viewer, preserving CMYK dot structure and one original-color subject.

## Metadata

- Collection: Academy Award Film Styles
- Medium: Cinematic HTML animation
- Best for: A low-contrast green newspaper photograph travels through a dusty microfilm viewer, preserving CMYK dot structure and one original-color subject.

## Signature Features

- Full-frame picture with no newspaper masthead or columns
- Visible four-angle process dots under green fluorescence
- Scan lines, rolling band, glass dust, one hair, and mild corner mask
- One halftoned subject retains subdued original color
- Horizontal film transport through dark frame gaps

## Style Prompt

Show only the reproduced photograph, not the newspaper page around it. Keep signs and labels within the image as solid printed lines while rendering tonal regions through visible cyan, magenta, yellow, and black screens at distinct process angles with slight registration error. Pass the composite through a microfilm reader: shift it to a low-contrast fluorescent green scale over a near-black green base while keeping the dot pattern recognizable. Permit the complete narrative lead to retain subdued original process color, with the same low contrast and halftone texture; convert every light source and all other color to green. Add fine horizontal scan lines, a pale band rolling through every few seconds, one soft lamp hotspot, dust on the glass, a single trapped hair, a very light rounded outer mask, and a small monospaced frame number. Push slowly within a frame. Change scenes only by transporting the film horizontally through a dark gap for half a second.

## Avoid

mastheads, article columns, full-color leakage, hard cuts, bright phosphor green, modern screen UI, and a clean defect-free viewport.
