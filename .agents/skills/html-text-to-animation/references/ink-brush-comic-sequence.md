# Ink-Brush Comic Sequence

## Overview

A readable comic narrative combines pressure-rich brush contours, wash atmosphere, panel rhythm, speech, and expressive action without losing the material behavior of ink. Choose this when expressive brush pressure, ink economy, and sequential comic pacing must coexist.

## Metadata

- Collection: Chinese Art Styles

## Signature Features

- Brush panels and gutters on warm paper
- Variable black contour, dry brush, and wet wash
- Sparse color or seal-red accent
- Speech balloons or captions integrated with brush composition
- Panel pushes, ink wipes, and limited internal motion

## Style Prompt

Lay out a short comic sequence with varied panel sizes and a clear reading path. Draw characters and key objects with pressure-rich brush contour, using dry split marks for speed, cloth, hair, and rough surfaces and diluted wash for atmosphere, distance, shadow, and emotional tone. Keep faces and gestures specific enough to carry dialogue. Reserve broad untouched paper and use color only as one restrained accent when needed. Letter concise speech balloons or captions cleanly in the user's language while making their shapes harmonize with the brush composition. Animate through measured pushes into panels, small held gestures, drifting wash, and transitions where an existing ink stroke or spreading wash covers the gutter and reveals the next scene. Return to the page often enough that panel structure remains meaningful.

## Avoid

- Choose **Single-Panel Ink-Wash Cartoon** for one distilled image, or **Halftone American Comic** for hard printed action.
Western superhero effects pasted onto ink, random splash transitions, illegible lettering, full animation that forgets the comic, every panel having the same size, grey wash over everything, and characters reduced to generic calligraphy blobs.
