# Hand-Tinted Sepia Portrait

## Overview

A turn-of-the-century silver photograph whose full sepia range supports loose translucent hand coloring and quiet frontal presence.

## Metadata

- Collection: Academy Award Film Styles
- Medium: Cinematic HTML animation
- Source type: visual style reference

## Signature Features

- Soft modeled bodies under upper-left light
- Complete sepia scale with substantial dark weight
- Midtone-heavy silver grain, soft focus, and mild halation
- Loose transparent dye that weakens in shadow
- Edge silvering, sparse foxing, scratches, and at most one cracked corner

## Style Prompt

Begin with realistically proportioned people and dimensional objects under a broad upper-left source, then translate the image into an early silver print. Maintain a full sepia scale from deep brown-black through warm mid-brown to cream, keeping at least one fifth of the frame near the dark end so age does not become low-contrast fog. Add slight optical softness, restrained halation, and silver grain strongest in the middle values. Brush transparent color loosely over the dry print, allowing it to cross contours and fade as it enters shadow. Give the central subject the densest muted warm dye, with only one or two small props receiving weaker color. Keep the pose frontal and documentary, as if the sitters have deliberately paused for the plate. Age the object gently with a vignette, cool metallic silvering along dark outer edges, a few fox marks, fine scratches, and no more than one damaged corner. Pan slowly across faces and held objects without breaking the stillness.

## Customizable Elements

Adapt the subject, setting, wording, duration, aspect ratio, and narrative emphasis. Adjust individual hues only within the palette roles defined above, and preserve the medium, composition, material behavior, and motion grammar that make the style recognizable.

## Avoid

blocky anatomy, digital selection-mask coloring, washed-out sepia, theatrical action poses, and overwhelming mold or tears.
