# Silent Aerial Orthochromatic

## Overview

Aerial action rendered as a worn 1920s silent print, with the camera physically attached to the moving subject.

## Metadata

- Collection: Academy Award Film Styles

## Signature Features

- Near-white skies, readable mid-grey terrain, and deep silhouette blacks
- Cream-amber tint confined to highlights while blacks remain neutral
- One story-critical subject may retain a muted rust accent
- Visible gate weave, exposure flutter, dust, hairline scratches, vignette, and an opening iris
- Rig-mounted, chase, and point-of-view shots linked by cloud or glare whites

## Style Prompt

Build smooth 3D forms, then grade them like an orthochromatic release print: red materials collapse toward black, the sky approaches cream-white, and terrain keeps distinct middle values. Reserve deep black for aircraft, machinery, figures, and other silhouettes rather than crushing the whole frame. Tint only the luminous range toward pale amber; never wash the image orange. Let one principal subject carry a restrained rust accent when narrative tracking needs it, while every practical light stays monochrome. Add fine grain, slight frame jitter, mild flicker, sparse dust, one or two vertical scratches, rounded gate corners, and a soft vignette. Open through a circular iris. Favor a camera bolted to the moving vehicle, with a sliver of the rig visible and vibration tied to its engine or impact; alternate with pursuit and subjective views. Cut through cloud, smoke, or overexposed sky.

## Avoid

modern motion blur, colored greys, and perfectly stable digital footage.
