# Misregistered Watercolor and Line

## Overview

A buoyant city-and-figure style in which transparent color and quick blue-black drawing deliberately refuse to align.

## Metadata

- Collection: Academy Award Film Styles
- Medium: Cinematic HTML animation
- Source type: visual style reference

## Signature Features

- Oversized cobalt, lemon, and rose washes displaced in one direction
- Wet rims, granulation, and clean violet or green overlaps
- Dense fast linework with flicked ends and overshot corners
- Looped strings for trees and clouds
- Paper-white skin and lively elastic figures

## Style Prompt

Lay transparent color before any drawing. Spread cobalt across sky and water, lemon across ground and light, and rose across warm surfaces, making each wash ten to forty percent larger than the object it implies and sliding all washes in one shared direction. Preserve wet edges, pooling, granulation, and luminous overlaps that mix to violet or green; never allow repeated layering to turn brown. Keep the focal subject's dedicated muted wash away from cobalt so it remains clean. Over this independent color field, rapidly draw the complete scene in fine blue-black ink: façades, windows, streets, furniture, figures, and small incidents. Let straight strokes overshoot corners, curved strokes taper into flicks, and trees or clouds form from strings of loops. Leave about a quarter of the sheet as exposed paper and use that paper for skin. Make bodies springy and gestures rhythmic. Animate with small pushes and beat-timed cuts while the ink and color maintain their offset.

## Customizable Elements

Adapt the subject, setting, wording, duration, aspect ratio, and narrative emphasis. Adjust individual hues only within the palette roles defined above, and preserve the medium, composition, material behavior, and motion grammar that make the style recognizable.

## Avoid

tracing color boundaries, sparse linework, muddy overlap, rigid anatomy, and computer-perfect wash masks.
