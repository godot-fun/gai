# Optical Kerning Ballet

## Overview

Letter pairs negotiate negative space through measured shifts, rotation, and scale while preserving word recognition.

## Metadata

- Collection: Typography and Language Systems
- Medium: HTML, SVG, Canvas, WebGL, or composited media
- Best for: type design, balance, visual rhythm, and refined title sequences
- Complexity: Moderate to advanced

## Signature Features

- Use a small set of large letter pairs on a neutral field, exposing sidebearings, optical gaps, and baseline alignment.
- Move one glyph at a time toward optical balance, compare before and after, and rotate pairs only when demonstrating shape interaction.
- For **Optical Kerning Ballet**, build hierarchy from the writing system itself: baseline, spacing, glyph anatomy, reading direction, and linguistic timing take precedence over illustration.

## Style Prompt

Create a polished HTML animation in the **Optical Kerning Ballet** style. Use a small set of large letter pairs on a neutral field, exposing sidebearings, optical gaps, and baseline alignment. Move one glyph at a time toward optical balance, compare before and after, and rotate pairs only when demonstrating shape interaction. Build hierarchy from glyph anatomy, spacing, reading direction, linguistic timing, and the specific encoding or word structure being demonstrated. Limit color to paper or screen neutrals plus one semantic accent; keep every required character readable at the intended viewing distance. Let reading order, linguistic structure, or encoding rules determine transitions instead of applying generic kinetic-type presets.

Adapt duration, aspect ratio, hierarchy, and annotation without changing supplied wording. Verify every character, pronunciation, encoding, and reading convention used by the effect.

## Customizable Elements

- Adjust type family, scale, spacing, line length, pacing, annotation depth, and the balance between primary reading and structural explanation.
- Change typographic scale, neutral surface, accent color, and motion amplitude while preserving valid glyphs, reading order, timing, and decodability.

## Avoid

- Avoid random dancing letters, uniform mathematical spacing, scaling glyphs to fake balance, excessive easing, or sacrificing word recognition.
- Do not invent characters, pronunciations, encodings, reading conventions, or linguistic relationships to complete the composition.
- Keep essential text readable and provide a stable reduced-motion state that preserves the final composition.
