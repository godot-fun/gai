# Limited-Palette Pixel Adventure

## Overview

A crisp low-resolution game world with authored sprites, stepped timing, tile-based depth, dithered shading, and a short readable quest. Choose this for side-view exploration and environmental storytelling constrained by a small authored palette.

## Metadata

- Collection: Motion Graphics Styles
- Medium: Cinematic HTML animation
- Best for: A crisp low-resolution game world with authored sprites, stepped timing, tile-based depth, dithered shading, and a short readable quest. Choose this for side-view exploration and environmental storytelling constrained by a small authored palette.

## Signature Features

- Small internal canvas enlarged by integer nearest-neighbor scaling
- Eight-to-thirty-two-color palette
- Eight-to-twelve-frame-per-second sprite animation
- Tile sets, parallax bands, dithered value transitions, and optional scan lines
- Clear player objective, pickup, obstacle, discovery, and end pose

## Style Prompt

Choose a low internal resolution and scale it by a whole number only. Author the environment from coherent tiles and the cast from dedicated sprite sheets with distinct idle, walk, jump, interact, and reaction frames. Restrict the full sequence to a deliberate eight-to-thirty-two-color palette and shade with pixel clusters or ordered dithering, never blur. Animate characters at a stepped eight-to-twelve-frame cadence while scrolling and parallax can update more frequently in integer pixel increments. Tell one compact quest: establish the location, reveal an objective, cross an obstacle, collect or unlock something, and finish on a readable victory, surprise, or title pose. Add small particles, water, grass, torches, or weather as repeating pixel cycles and optional restrained CRT lines.

## Avoid

- Choose **16-Bit Console RPG** when menus and turn-based systems matter, or **Top-Down Pixel Neon City** for overhead urban motion.
anti-aliasing, subpixel placement, oversized random pixels, gradients, inconsistent sprite scale, palette expansion per scene, smooth tweening, and game UI borrowed from a known title.
